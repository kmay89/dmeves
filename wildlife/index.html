The law, in plain words

What Ohio and federal rules say about each animal a Medina County finder is likely to call about. A summary, not legal advice. Citations are under Ops desk → Sources.

Wildlife belongs to the state. No one may keep, move or care for a wild animal except as the law allows. There is no "finder's" exception, so get it to a permit holder or ODNR the same day. ORC 1531.02
Rehabilitation needs a state permit. Category I covers orphans of common species; raccoons, skunks, foxes, bats, raptors and deer are excluded. Category II, after three years, covers most of the rest, with rabies-vector species still needing the Chief's approval. The permit is free; records are kept two years. OAC 1501:31-25-03 · ORC 1533.08
Birds are federal too. Nearly every native bird needs a federal migratory bird permit on top of the Ohio one. Only starlings, house sparrows and pigeons are unprotected. MBTA · USFWS 3-200-10b · ORC 1533.07
Nuisance animals have their own rule. You may live-trap one on your property and hold it up to 24 hours. A trapped raccoon, skunk, beaver, coyote, red fox or opossum must be released on site or euthanized, never relocated. OAC 1501:31-15-03
Bites get reported. Any mammal bite goes to the Medina County Health Department within 24 hours. A bat in a room where someone slept counts as an exposure: contain it, don't release it, call. OAC 3701-3-28

A realistic week for one person during baby season

Assumes a day job, Category I small mammals only, and a hard cap of about ten animals in care. Baby season (April to August) overlaps summer break, which is the only reason this works for a teacher.

BlockWhatHours / week
Early morningFeedings, weights, meds, cleaning8
MiddayTexts from finders; triage photos; schedule drop-offs2
EveningIntakes, feedings, enclosure work12
WeekendDeep clean, supply run, vet drop-offs, 15 minutes of ledger6
Total at peak · about 8 to 10 animals in care28

Outside baby season the same list is 5 to 8 hours a week. The intake cap is the whole plan: no neonates that need overnight feedings until there is a second trained feeder, and a firm "no, but here is who to call" for everything the permit doesn't cover.
