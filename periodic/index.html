A table that checks itself

Every value here starts from a curated snapshot — and the page quietly re-verifies it against the PubChem Periodic Table (NIH/NCBI, which carries IUPAC & NIST data) about once a week, right in your browser. If the chemistry world revises a value, this table follows without anyone touching the code.

Where the numbers come from

  • Atomic weights — IUPAC/CIAAW Standard Atomic Weights 2021, abridged to five digits. Bracketed values [268] are the mass number of the longest-lived isotope.
  • Ionization energies & electron affinities — NIST Atomic Spectra Database (kJ/mol).
  • Melting, boiling, density, abundance — CRC Handbook of Chemistry & Physics reference values (K; g/cm³, gases g/L at STP; crustal mg/kg).
  • Covalent radii — Cordero et al. 2008; beyond Z=96, Pyykkö 2009 predictions.
  • Electron configurations — NIST, including the famous anomalies (Cr, Cu, Nb, Pd, La, Lr…).

Honesty notes

  • For elements beyond ≈ fermium (Z ≥ 100), bulk properties have never been measured — values marked ≈ predicted come from theory, and a few atoms per year is all we get.
  • Carbon and arsenic sublime at ordinary pressure — they skip the liquid phase.
  • The 32-column wide form follows the IUPAC provisional arrangement (Lu and Lr below Y). The familiar 18-column form keeps La–Lu and Ac–Lr as footnote rows.

Privacy

No accounts, no analytics, no cookies. The only network request this page ever makes is the optional data check to PubChem; its result is cached in your own browser.
